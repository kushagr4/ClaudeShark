# V2.3: the tempo constant

**Date:** 2026-09-04. **Branch:** `v2.2-development`.
**Baseline / control:** `champions/v2_1_kingpawn` (the V2 champion,
`TEMPO = 8`).
**Candidates:** `champions/v2_1_kingpawn_tempo16`, `_tempo24`, `_tempo32`,
each produced by `tools.make_time_variants --module cs_constants.py --constant
TEMPO`, which refuses to write a variant unless exactly one line of exactly one
file differs.

This section is written **before** any candidate result is read, so that the
selection rule cannot be adjusted to fit the answer.

## 1. The observation that motivates it

`tools/daily/calibrate.py` compared the engine's own scores with the oracle on
35,202 positions taken from every ply of four annotated 200-game corpora — an
unselected sample, not conditioned on the engine having erred, on the phase, or
on the class.

The evaluator is exactly colour symmetric, so its white's-point-of-view static
score can be written as `P + s * TEMPO`, where `s` is +1 when White is to move
and −1 when Black is. Comparing that with the oracle's white's-view score and
splitting the sample by side to move gives an estimator of the tempo error that
does not depend on the rest of the evaluation being right, because the
positional part `P - P_true` has the same mean in both halves of a
colour-balanced corpus:

    tempo error = (bias when White is to move - bias when Black is to move) / 2

| phase | n (White to move) | n (Black to move) | bias, White to move | bias, Black to move | implied tempo error | implied correct tempo |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| opening (18-24) | 1,140 | 1,115 | −33.5 | +79.7 | **−56.6** | 64.6 |
| middlegame (10-17) | 4,070 | 4,090 | −45.8 | +46.4 | **−46.1** | 54.1 |
| late (4-9) | 7,686 | 7,706 | −50.1 | +18.2 | **−34.2** | 42.2 |
| endgame (0-3) | 4,714 | 4,681 | −66.9 | −26.8 | **−20.0** | 28.0 |
| all | 17,610 | 17,592 | −52.5 | +16.7 | **−34.6** | 42.6 |

The shipped constant is **8**. The static evaluation therefore under-values
having the move by roughly 35 centipawns on average and by nearly 60 in the
opening.

## 2. The reason this is not a foregone conclusion

The same estimator applied to the **depth-6 root score** instead of the static
score shows that the search already recovers most of it:

| phase | implied tempo error at the root |
|---|---|
| opening | −16.1 |
| middlegame | −13.2 |
| late | −2.1 |
| endgame | +3.3 |
| all | **−4.1** |

Six plies of search see the value of the move for themselves. What is left
unrecovered is 13 to 16 centipawns in the opening and middlegame and nothing at
all in the endgame — and in the endgame the root is already very slightly on
the other side of zero, so a large increase would over-correct exactly where
the engine plays most of its decisive moves.

This is why the candidate family is centred well below the 42.6 the static
residual implies, and why the experiment is worth running rather than simply
applying: the honest prior is that the right answer might be **8**.

## 3. Pre-registered design

**Family.** Three values, fixed before any result: `TEMPO = 16` (SMALL),
`24` (MEDIUM), `32` (LARGE), against the shipped `8`. No other value will be
tried, and no value outside this family will be introduced afterwards.

**What is measured.** Move quality, not calibration. The user's standing rule
is that a calibration change must never be scored as a quality improvement, and
a tempo change is precisely the kind that could make the numbers agree with
Stockfish while playing no better. So the selection statistic is: each variant
searches each position at fixed depth 6, the oracle scores the position after
its chosen move at 1,000,000 nodes, and the loss is the oracle's own first
choice minus that, floored at zero (`tools/daily/variants.py`).

**Population.** A phase-stratified, unselected sample from the same four
annotated corpora — not the error positions, because a tempo change acts on
every move, not only on mistakes. Positions are split **by source cluster**
(even diagnostic, odd validation), never by row.

**Selection rule, fixed in advance.** The variant with the lowest mean loss on
the **diagnostic** half wins, and only if it beats `TEMPO = 8` there by at
least 3 centipawns of mean loss; otherwise the experiment stops at "no variant
selected" and nothing is carried forward. The validation half is read exactly
once, after the choice is frozen.

**Gates.** A selected variant then goes through Gate 1 against the champion on
the calibration set, the blind-win and false-win suites, the conversion suite,
the 240-position competition-like suite, tactics, and the deterministic
fingerprint, followed by 200 paired fixed-depth games at Gate 2 — reported with
the number of *informative* clusters, after the V2.2a match showed that a
headline W/D/L can rest on a single one.

**Cost.** Zero. The tempo is already added on every evaluation; only its value
changes. Node counts will differ because the tree differs, and that is a
consequence, not a cost.
