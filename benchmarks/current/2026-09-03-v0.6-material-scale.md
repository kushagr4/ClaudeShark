# v0.6 material scale: one parameter, fitted honestly

Date: 2026-09-03
Baseline: `champions/v0_5_2_correctness` (snapshot `6eee6bd9b5e58e29`)
Candidate: `champions/v0_6_material_scale` (snapshot `5c02726caf077231`)
Rejected candidate: `champions/v0_6_material_fit` (snapshot `6251ae78018d2672`)
Corpus: `corpus/competition_like_v1.jsonl` (`competition_like-v1`, declared hash `6a8111f22f9ea393`)

## What was tested

The hypothesis was that the ten-parameter material fit does not justify
replacing ten numbers, because the relative piece ratios are already about
right and what is actually wrong is that material as a whole is underweighted
against the PeSTO piece-square tables. One parameter:

    material_contribution_new = s * material_contribution_current

Piece-square tables, bishop pair, tempo and the entire search are untouched.
The scaled values are rounded to integers once, when the candidate is built,
so the shipped change is a constant substitution with no runtime cost.

## The result that matters most is methodological

The naive version of this fit reports an 11.4% loss improvement and runs
monotonically off the edge of any grid you give it. That number is almost
entirely an artefact.

Scaling material makes the evaluator's *units* larger. The Texel sigmoid scale
K converts centipawns to expected score, so a fixed K rewards any change that
inflates the evaluator whether or not it improves a single move. Refitting K
on the training split at every s separates the two effects:

| fit | best val ES-MSE | improvement |
|---|---|---|
| material scale, K fixed | - | -11.4% |
| material scale, K refitted | 0.04287 | **-2.6%** |
| whole-evaluator scale, K free | 0.04402 | flat |

The whole-evaluator control is the proof. A uniform scale of every evaluation
term is statically decision-invariant -- argmax does not care about units --
and with K free its curve is flat to four decimal places (spread 0.00009)
across the entire grid, exactly as theory requires: for a uniform scale, s and
K are the same parameter. So of the naive 11.4 points, about 8.9 were
calibration and about 2.6 were chess.

Selected s = **1.47** (K = 148), chosen on validation only, with a broad
plateau: every s in [1.29, 1.71] is within 1% of the best. This is not a
fragile optimum.

The uniform-scale control was also run through the real engine, where search
margins stay in centipawns and so do not scale with it: 48 of 240 moves
changed, 20 better and 22 worse, robust loss -0.6, agreement -1.3%. A wash.
The search margins are not materially miscalibrated at this scale, so the
material-scale result can legitimately be read as evaluation rather than as
an accidental margin retune.

## Why the scalar was preferred over the ten-parameter fit

The two candidates are nearly identical except in one number: middlegame
queen, 1507 for the scalar against 1210 for the fit. The ten-parameter model
is, in substance, "scale everything by about 1.5 but hold the queen back".

The evidence between them is genuinely mixed. The fit is better on the
competition-like suite (external robust 25.8 against 30.0); the scalar is
better on the stress suite (23.4 against 26.1); head to head they differ on 44
of 240 positions with the fit better on 18 and the scalar better on 16. That
is not the significant deterministic win the brief required before the
ten-parameter candidate could be played.

The widened-bound diagnostic explains why the fit leans this way, and it is
not a chess reason. Lifting the [0.6x, 1.5x] band with K refitted:

| bounds | lambda | on bound | val ES-MSE | MG queen |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| [0.6, 1.5] | 0 | 3/10 | 0.04234 | 723 |
| [0.5, 2.0] | 0 | 1/10 | 0.04200 | 546 |
| [0.4, 2.5] | 0 | 1/10 | 0.04188 | 410 |
| [0.3, 3.0] | 0 | 0/10 | 0.04188 | 388 |
| [0.25, 4.0] | 0 | 0/10 | 0.04188 | 388 |

The middlegame queen collapses from 1025 to 388 and settles there, and the
lambda sweep selects zero regularisation at every width. This is the same
large negative queen correction the previous session diagnosed as an artefact
of the fitting setup rather than a chess fact. The artefact was never removed;
it was held back by the bound. Inside the bound the fit still leans the same
way -- pawn and knight pinned to the 1.5x ceiling, queen held to 1.18x.

So the ten-parameter candidate's extra margin rides on a component already
known to be an artefact. The one-parameter candidate carries no such
liability, has one degree of freedom instead of ten, and sits on a broad
plateau. It is the candidate that goes to the arena.

## Mechanism: what the scale actually changes

Structural breakdown of the scalar against baseline over all 240 positions,
robust loss by tag (lower is better).

Improved -- material judgement and closed positions:

| tag | n | A | B | delta |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| exchange_imbalance | 18 | 38.7 | 19.1 | -19.6 |
| exposed_king | 54 | 57.9 | 42.8 | -15.1 |
| closed_centre | 24 | 44.1 | 34.5 | -9.6 |
| locked_pawn_chain | 31 | 40.5 | 31.0 | -9.5 |
| hanging_piece | 45 | 38.3 | 29.0 | -9.2 |
| king_in_centre | 48 | 31.9 | 23.4 | -8.6 |

Regressed -- passed-pawn structures, without exception:

| tag | n | A | B | delta |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| advanced_passer | 33 | 42.8 | 57.9 | +15.1 |
| connected_passers | 26 | 41.4 | 56.4 | +15.0 |
| opposite_side_castling | 21 | 32.1 | 47.0 | +14.9 |
| carlsbad | 22 | 16.1 | 25.9 | +9.8 |
| queenside_majority | 46 | 48.2 | 56.7 | +8.5 |
| protected_passer | 41 | 37.6 | 45.3 | +7.7 |

This is coherent and it is the honest cost of the change. The engine has no
passed-pawn term at all; its only passed-pawn awareness is the rank bonus
inside the pawn piece-square table. Scaling material up relatively devalues
exactly that, so the positions where a pawn's value comes from where it is
rather than what it is get worse. The improvements are concentrated where the
opposite is true -- exchanges, closed positions, hanging material -- which is
what an underweighted-material hypothesis predicts.

Persistent failures: the scalar fixes cl-125 (225 cp to 121 cp, `c4e3`
becomes `c4a5`). The ten-parameter fit fixes cl-097 instead (334 cp to 22 cp).
Neither fixes the other five.

## Decision: REVERT

Production was never modified, so the revert is a no-op: `cs_constants.py`
still carries the production values and the only engine difference against
`champions/v0_5_2_correctness` remains the dormant king-safety hook in
`cs_eval.py`. Both candidate snapshots are retained under `champions/` as
evidence, not as releases.

### What this result actually shows

This is the most useful thing the session produced, and it is a negative.

The candidate passed every deterministic gate the brief set. It had a better
robust loss on the leakage-free external suite, fewer serious errors, no
increase in catastrophic errors, a clearly better stress suite, 16/16 tactics,
no loss of sacrifice-finding, zero runtime cost, a coherent structural
mechanism, one degree of freedom, a properly cross-validated fit with K
refitted, and a broad plateau. It then lost 40 Elo.

So the deterministic move-quality suite did not predict playing strength. A
suite scores single moves against an oracle and averages; a game compounds a
systematic bias over eighty moves and punishes it. A 1.47x material scale is
exactly such a bias.

**Correction (2026-09-03 post-mortem):** this record originally said only 1 of
69 decisive games saw a promotion. That was a measurement error -- the check
counted queens on the board rather than queens gained, which misses every
promotion made after the original queens were traded. The correct figure is
**28 of 69**, and 44 of the 46 losses end with the candidate about nine
pawn-units behind at mate with twelve or fewer pieces left. The losses are
ground-down endgame conversions. See
`benchmarks/current/2026-09-03-v0.6-postmortem.md` for the full analysis; the
paragraph below is left as originally written.

The passed-pawn regression visible in the structural breakdown is *not* the
mechanism, and it is worth being explicit that the obvious explanation failed:
only 1 of 69 decisive games saw a promotion at all, and the losses are not
concentrated in long games. The damage is broadest in the 60-99 ply band
(37.9%), which is middlegame play, and it is present with both colours. The
honest summary is that the bias hurts general middlegame judgement in a way
this session did not isolate further.

The methodological lesson stands independently of the chess: a one-move
oracle-agreement suite is a screening tool, not a strength gate. Nothing should
be promoted on it. That is also why the brief's rule to run the arena before
believing a deterministic win was right, and why the reverse rule -- never use
games to rescue a bad deterministic result -- is not symmetric with it.
